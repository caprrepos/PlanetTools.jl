PlanetDustUtils

PlanetTools.PlanetDustUtils — Module
PlanetTools.PlanetDustUtils

Submodule of PlanetTools with utilities for mineral dust, currently the size distribution of emitted dust from brittle fragmentation theory [Kok, 2011]. Load it with using PlanetTools.PlanetDustUtils.

source
PlanetTools.PlanetDustUtils.calc_fractional_dust_masses_bft — Method
calc_fractional_dust_masses_bft(diameter_bounds; kwargs...) -> Vector{Float64}

Return the fraction of emitted dust mass in each size bin between consecutive diameter_bounds, from the volume size distribution of brittle fragmentation theory [Kok, 2011, Eq. 6],

dV/dD ∝ [1 + erf(ln(D / D̄s) / (√2 ln σs))] exp(-(D / λ)³).

D is the particle diameter, as are the bin bounds of the ModelE sectional dust scheme; do not pass radii, such as the effective radii used for radiative calculations. The bin fractions are integrals of this distribution over the bins. Mass fractions equal volume fractions, i.e. the particle density is assumed to be independent of size.

Arguments

  • diameter_bounds::AbstractVector{<:Real}: bin bounds as particle diameters (not radii) in µm, strictly increasing, diameter_bounds[1] ≥ 0. The last bound may be Inf.

Keywords

  • median_diameter::Real=3.4: median diameter by volume of the parent soil, D̄s, in µm.
  • geometric_sd::Real=3.0: geometric standard deviation of the parent soil, σs, > 1.
  • crack_length::Real=12.0: side crack propagation length, λ, in µm.
  • normalize::Bool=true: if true, the fractions sum to 1 over the bins; if false, they are fractions of the total emitted mass over all sizes.
  • method::Symbol=:integral: :integral integrates numerically; :midpoint multiplies dV/dD at the geometric bin mean by the bin width (earlier implementation, kept to reproduce old results; inaccurate for wide bins).

The default parameters are those of Kok [2011] for arid soils.

Returns

  • Vector{Float64}: one fraction per bin, length(diameter_bounds) - 1 values.

Throws

  • ArgumentError: if there are fewer than two bounds, the bounds are not strictly increasing or are negative, a parameter is out of range, or the distribution is zero in all bins.

References

  • Kok, J. F. (2011), A scaling theory for the size distribution of emitted dust aerosols suggests climate models underestimate the size of the global dust cycle, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. U.S.A., 108(3), 1016–1021, doi:10.1073/pnas.1014798108.

Examples

julia> f = calc_fractional_dust_masses_bft([0.2, 2, 4, 8, 16]);

julia> round.(f; digits=3)
4-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.045
 0.143
 0.388
 0.425

julia> sum(calc_fractional_dust_masses_bft([0, Inf]; normalize=false)) ≈ 1
true
